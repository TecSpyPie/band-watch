HamShack privacy

Back to HamShack

The short version

HamShack has no accounts, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking. Your settings, your log and your callsign stay on your own device. The few things that do reach our server are there to show you spots, run the chat and stop abuse, and they are not sold or shared.

This page covers the HamShack website (band-watch.ample.run), the HamShack app on Google Play (which is the same website in an app window) and the Windows app. Last updated 28 November 2026.

Kept on your device only

These are saved in your browser's own storage on your phone or PC. They never leave it unless one of the points further down says so. Clearing the site's data (or uninstalling the app) removes them.

  • Your callsign and locator, and all your settings (bands, colours, panels, radio and decoder choices).
  • Your logbook, Wanted list, repeaters and SSTV pictures.
  • Your QRZ Logbook API key, if you add one.

Your location: if you press the button to find your locator, your device works out the locator itself. Your exact position is never sent anywhere. You can always type your locator instead.

The microphone or sound input: if you start a decoder (FT8, CW, SSTV and so on), the sound is decoded on your device. It is never recorded or sent anywhere.

What reaches the HamShack server

  • Spots: the first four characters of your locator (for example IO93), to fetch the PSK Reporter reports for your area. Answers are kept for 5 minutes so PSK Reporter isn't asked too often.
  • Who heard me: your callsign, if you use it, to ask PSK Reporter who heard you. Callsigns are public on the air.
  • Chat: the callsign you type and your message. Messages are public, and the newest 200 in each room are kept for 48 hours, then deleted. Your internet address is held in memory for a minute to stop flooding; if you report a message, only a scrambled form of your address is kept, so nobody can tell who reported it.
  • QRZ.com: if you sign in to QRZ, your QRZ username and password are passed straight to QRZ once and never stored. The server keeps QRZ's session key in memory for up to a day so lookups work, and your browser only holds a cookie that points to it. Signing out removes it. Logbook uploads pass your key through to QRZ and the server doesn't keep it.
  • Ordinary web logs: like every website, the company hosting HamShack may briefly keep standard request logs (internet address, time, page asked for) to keep the service running and safe. HamShack itself doesn't log who you are.

Other sites your device talks to

Some parts of HamShack load things straight from other services. Those services see your internet address, as with any web page, and have their own privacy policies.

  • OpenStreetMap (map tiles) and unpkg.com (the map's code), when you open a map.
  • CelesTrak (satellite orbits, at most once every 2 hours) and NASA SDO (the picture of the sun).
  • wspr.live, if you use the antenna comparison with WSPR reports for your callsign.
  • Any web receiver (WebSDR, KiwiSDR), QRZ page or other link you choose to open.

What HamShack never does

  • No analytics, adverts, advertising IDs or trackers.
  • No selling or sharing of anything about you.
  • No names, email addresses or phone numbers asked for.
  • No contacts, photos or files read from your device.

Questions or a chat message to remove

Any chat message can be reported with its Report button; when three people report it, it comes down for everyone, and every message is gone after 48 hours anyway. For anything else, open an issue on github.com/TecSpyPie/band-watch. HamShack by M0GLP & M3VRV is free and open source (MIT licence), so you can read exactly what it does.

Children: HamShack is meant for radio amateurs and isn't aimed at children.
