        Listen

        Tune a public web receiver. Click the scope, the band plan or a frequency in Band activity to tune it there.

        Plug in the dongle and start rtl_tcp from the RTL-SDR Blog drivers (they support the Blog V4), for example rtl_tcp -a 127.0.0.1. Band Watch connects to it when you press Listen, and in the Windows app starts rtl_tcp itself if it isn't running (put rtl_tcp.exe in C:\BandWatch, or say where it is in Settings). The V4 tunes 500 kHz to 1,766 MHz; below 28.8 MHz it switches in its built-in HF upconverter by itself. Only tick Bias tee if an active antenna or preamp needs power from the dongle.

        Find more receivers: KiwiSDR map · WebSDR list. Most digital modes (FT8, WSPR and so on) sound like tones; decode them with WSJT-X or fldigi.

        The Listen panel is in its own window.

        Decoders: FT8, FT4, WSPR, CW

        Decodes what your receiver hears, like WSJT-X. Your PC clock must be right to within a second.

              Radio control (Yaesu, Icom, Kenwood)
              
      
              Plug your radio into this PC with its USB cable and install the maker's USB driver first
        (Yaesu and Icom use the Silicon Labs CP210x driver; Kenwood has its own virtual COM port driver).
        Band Watch then tunes the radio when you click a spot, the band scope, the band plan or a decoder's Tune button,
        and follows the radio when you turn its dial. On a Yaesu, pick the Enhanced COM Port when the browser asks.
        Close other programs that use the radio's CAT port (WSJT-X, a logger) first, or use their rig sharing.
        Needs Chrome or Edge.
              
      
              
        
        
              
        
        
              
          The radio's CI-V address. If you changed it in the radio's menu, Band Watch follows the radio when it answers.

                  Your locator
                  
      
                  Band Watch fetches spots for your grid square and measures distances from your locator.
      A 6-character locator such as IO93fa pins you down to about 5 km, so distances and the map are more accurate than with the 4-character square.

                    Band Watch by M3vrv. Spots come from PSK Reporter:
  stations in your grid square heard by others, and stations heard in your grid square, over the last 30 minutes.
  Solar numbers come from NOAA SWPC.
  Sunspot regions, X-rays, solar wind and NOAA scales also come from NOAA SWPC.
  Status is judged from spot counts: Open means 15 or more stations, Marginal means 5 to 14, Closed means fewer.
  The scope is drawn from those spot reports, not from a receiver. Lines on the main map are straight; the station window shows the true great-circle path.
  Listening uses public web receivers run by volunteers; Band Watch doesn't control what they hear.
