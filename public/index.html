        Listen

        Tune a public web receiver. Click the scope, the band plan or a frequency in Band activity to tune it there.

        Plug in the dongle and press Listen. The Windows app comes with rtl_tcp (from the RTL-SDR Blog drivers, which support the Blog V4) and starts it for you. Elsewhere, start rtl_tcp yourself, for example rtl_tcp -a 127.0.0.1, and Band Watch connects to it. The V4 tunes 500 kHz to 1,766 MHz; below 28.8 MHz it switches in its built-in HF upconverter by itself. Only tick Bias tee if an active antenna or preamp needs power from the dongle.

        Find more receivers: KiwiSDR map · WebSDR list. Most digital modes (FT8, WSPR and so on) sound like tones; decode them with WSJT-X or fldigi.

        The Listen panel is in its own window.

          Transmit

            How to transmit, and keeping it clean
            1. Connect your radio (Connect radio), then click Tune to FT8 so it's on the FT8 frequency in DATA-USB.
            2. Start decoding. In Sound to the radio, choose the radio's USB sound (FT-991A: USB Audio CODEC).
            3. Set low power on the radio. Click Tune and raise Level until the radio's ALC meter just starts to move, then back it off a little. Too much level splatters.
            4. Click Reply on a CQ (or Call CQ), then Enable TX. Band Watch sends the usual messages and stops after the 73.

            Band Watch only transmits when Enable TX is on, inside an amateur band, and stops after 6 overs with no answer. Stop TX or Esc stops at once. Your power and where in the band your licence lets you transmit are for you to set. Your PC clock must be right to within a second.

            UTCStationTypeDistanceDetails

            APRS needs an antenna for 2 m on your RTL-SDR. In the UK and Europe it's on 144.800 MHz FM; the ISS's digipeater is on 145.825 MHz FM when it passes over (see the Satellites panel for when).

            Pictures start by themselves when their VIS code is heard, in any of these modes: Martin M1/M2, Scottie S1/S2/DX, Robot 36/72, PD 50/90/120/160/180/240/290, Wraase SC2-180, Pasokon P3/P5/P7. Band Watch straightens slanted pictures by itself. On HF, SSTV is around 14.230 MHz USB (20 m) or 7.171 MHz LSB (40 m); the ISS sends SSTV on 145.800 MHz FM during its events.

              Digital voice
              
      DMR, BrandMeister and Yaesu System Fusion (add-on)
    
              
    
              Band Watch can't make or send DMR or Fusion (C4FM) voice itself: they use a patented voice codec
      (AMBE) that only radios and hotspots have. Band Watch can put your Yaesu radio on C4FM, and take you to the right pages.
              
    
              Fusion (C4FM) on your radio
              
    
              
    
              
      
      
    
              
    
              
    
              This only sets the frequency and C4FM mode (2 m and 70 cm). Band Watch never transmits here:
      you talk on your own microphone. For a repeater or WIRES-X node, use a memory channel on the radio with its shift and tone.
              
    
              BrandMeister (DMR)
              
    
              
      Hoseline: listen in your browser
      Talkgroups
      Last heard
    
              
    
              Talkgroups to try: 235 UK wide, 91 worldwide, 92 Europe, 9990 Parrot
      (plays you back, to test your audio). On Hoseline, pick the talkgroup to listen.
              
    
              DMR over the internet
              
    
              BrandMeister links DMR repeaters and hotspots all over the world through the internet.
      To listen, you need no radio: Hoseline plays the talkgroups in your browser.
      To talk, you need something with the AMBE voice codec. The usual way is a DMR radio and a hotspot
      (Pi-Star or WPSD) on your home internet. Apps such as DroidStar can connect too, with an AMBE USB stick
      (e.g. ThumbDV or DVstick 30). Either way you need your licence and a DMR ID from RadioID.net.
      Band Watch can't carry the voice itself.

                  Antenna A/B
                  
      How to run a fair test
    
                  
    
                  Compare two antennas (or before and after a change) using real reports of your own WSPR, FT8 or FT4 signal. This only
      reads reports: it never controls your radio and never transmits. You send with your own software and switch antennas yourself.
                  
    
                  
      
      
    
                  
    
                  
      
      
      
    
                  
    
                  
    
                    
    
                    
      Add a period by typing the times
      
                    
        
        
        
        
      
                    
      
                    
    
                    
    
                    
      
      
      Looks up reports of your callsign: WSPR on wspr.live (up to 7 days), FT8 and FT4 on PSK Reporter (last 24 hours).
    
                    
    
                    
    
                    
      FT8 and FT4: keep Band Watch open while you swap antennas. It collects the reports every 5 minutes, and matches build up as stations hear you on each antenna.

                    Radio control (Yaesu, Icom, Kenwood, ATS Mini, Malahit)
                    
      
                    Plug your radio into this PC with its USB cable and install the maker's USB driver first
        (Yaesu and Icom use the Silicon Labs CP210x driver; Kenwood has its own virtual COM port driver).
        Band Watch then tunes the radio when you click a spot, the band scope, the band plan or a decoder's Tune button,
        and follows the radio when you turn its dial. On a Yaesu, pick the Enhanced COM Port when the browser asks.
        Close other programs that use the radio's CAT port (WSJT-X, a logger) first, or use their rig sharing.
        Needs Chrome or Edge.
        Yaesu FT-991A: pick Yaesu below, 4800 baud (menu 031 CAT RATE), and pick the Enhanced COM Port.
        Icom IC-7300: on the radio, MENU > SET > Connectors > CI-V: CI-V USB Port Unlink from [REMOTE],
        CI-V USB Baud Rate Auto, CI-V Transceive ON. Then pick Icom and IC-7300 below, 115200 baud, press Connect radio
        and choose the Silicon Labs CP210x port.
        Step-by-step help for each radio: Help → Connect your radio.
        ATS Mini: no driver needed. On the radio, set Settings > USB Port to Ad hoc,
        then pick ATS Mini below and press Connect radio.
        Malahit DSP2: plug it in, pick Malahit below, press Connect radio and choose its Malahit CAT port.
                    
      
                    
        
        
                    
        
        
                    
          The radio's CI-V address. If you changed it in the radio's menu, Band Watch follows the radio when it answers.

                          Band Watch by M3VRV & King Coder. Spots come from PSK Reporter:
  stations in your grid square heard by others, and stations heard in your grid square, over the last 30 minutes.
  Solar numbers come from NOAA SWPC.
  Sunspot regions, X-rays, solar wind and NOAA scales also come from NOAA SWPC.
  Status is judged from spot counts: Open means 15 or more stations, Marginal means 5 to 14, Closed means fewer.
  The scope is drawn from those spot reports, not from a receiver. On the map, the Map view draws curved great-circle paths and the Bowl view draws simple straight lines.
  Listening uses public web receivers run by volunteers; Band Watch doesn't control what they hear.
